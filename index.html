04 / Method

How I work.

S.01

Discover

I start with the people and the paper trail: stakeholder conversations, written requirements, and the coordination gaps the team already complains about. At Smart Marketing Methods the recurring gap was task ownership.

Artifact: written requirements + problem statement
S.02

Define

I scope in writing: who it serves, what done looks like, what is out. Async written updates kept the Germany team aligned without adding meetings.

Artifact: PRD, success metrics
S.03

Design

I sketch the smallest version with the team and cut the rest. We shipped task assignment and progress tracking before lower-priority interface work.

Artifact: flows + prototypes
S.04

Deliver

I run the Trello board in priority order, unblock engineers daily, and ship in small, reviewable pieces. Eight features shipped this way.

Artifact: shipped releases
S.05

Measure

After each release I checked the async updates for the coordination gap we set out to close, then decided: iterate, keep, or kill.

Artifact: readout + decision

PRD excerpt · sample · same product as the roadmap

District-level flood alerts

Problem

Flood warnings arrive late or not at all in the districts that need them most. Authorities and the public watch different channels, so nobody shares one picture of what is coming.

Goals and non-goals

Goal: a district gets a warning early enough to act on it. Non-goal: predicting exact water levels street by street.

Users

District authorities coordinating response; residents in flood-prone districts, including low-bandwidth and non-English readers.

Requirements
  • P0: district-level alert thresholds, alert dispatch to app, SMS fallback.
  • P1: Urdu and Sindhi alert copy, rainfall forecast overlay.
Success metrics

Warning lead time per district; share of alerts acknowledged.

Risks

False alarms erode trust faster than late ones.

Open questions

Who owns the threshold numbers: the model or the district office?
